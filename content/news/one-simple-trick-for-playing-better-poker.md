<p>Logic is the cornerstone of good poker strategy. To be a winning poker player, you need to be able to synthesize your mathematical <a href="news/the-one-thing-all-winning-players-have-in-common.html">studies</a> with an <a href="news/psychological-player-profiling.html">understanding of psychological player profiling</a>. There’s a lot that goes into it.</p>

<p>Context provides reasons for taking line A over line B. No matter what the situation, there’s always an optimal play—sometimes its pushing all in, and other times its folding.</p>

<p>Though there’s no golden rule for knowing whether to do one thing over another, there is one simple trick that you can use to make better decisions. Always have a reason.</p>

<p><strong>The Good and the Bad</strong></p>

<p>There are some good reasons for making a play. If you’re getting great <a href="news/poker-fundamentals-pot-odds.html">pot odds</a> on a nut draw, then you should probably call. If you’re playing <a href="news/playing-against-nits.html">against a nit</a> who’s showing weakness, go ahead and fire that big bluff.</p>

<p>The important thing to remember is that you’re verbalizing to yourself why you’re taking these actions. There is a justification behind everything you do. You have a reason, so, even if you’re wrong, you can learn from your play.</p>

<p>What you shouldn’t do, on the other hand, is make plays just because you feel like it or because you’re bored. These aren’t good reasons! Don’t open a mediocre hand just because you want to get in the pot.</p>

<p>Whatever you do, don’t play thoughtlessly. Throwing chips into the pot without having a reason is a surefire way to lose chips and not improve.</p>

<p><strong>Deviating from Your Strategy</strong></p>

<p>The most important time to think through all the reasons for making a play is when you deviate from your default strategy. For instance, limping is generally a bad play. If you do decide to limp, however, you need a good reason for doing so.</p>

<p>Perhaps you’re in late position with a mediocre hand and the blinds are weak players who won’t pressure you—see the flop and maybe you can extract some chips from them if you hit.</p>

<p>The key here is that you’re not just going to limp because you’re bored. The deviation happens for a very good reason.</p>

<p>Try this exercise in your next game. Whenever you take any action, ask yourself why you’re doing so. This will go a long way towards making you a better poker player.</p>
