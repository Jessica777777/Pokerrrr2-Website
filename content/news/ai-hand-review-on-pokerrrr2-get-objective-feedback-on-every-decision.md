<h2>Stop Guessing Why You Lost. Start Knowing.</h2>

<p>Most poker players replay losing hands in their head after a session and come to the wrong conclusion. They blame the cards, the timing, or the opponent — when the real issue was a decision made three streets earlier that they'll never review.</p>

<p>Pokerrrr 2's <strong>AI Hand Review</strong> changes that. After your private Holdem or PLO session, every hand you played is logged in your Game Record. Pull up any hand and the AI breaks down each action — what you did, what the EV-maximizing line was, and what the gap between the two cost you.</p>

<p>No guessing. No selective memory. Objective feedback on every decision you made.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Action-by-action breakdown</strong> — pre-flop through river, every bet, call, fold, and raise evaluated</li>
<li><strong>EV feedback</strong> — where your decision gained or lost expected value compared to the optimal line</li>
<li><strong>Mistake identification</strong> — specific moments where a different action would have been more profitable</li>
<li><strong>Improvement direction</strong> — not just "you were wrong" but what the better play was and why</li>
</ul>

<p>The goal isn't to tell you that you lost a hand. It's to show you <em>why</em> — and give you something concrete to work on next session.</p>

<h2>Why EV Matters More Than Results</h2>

<p>Winning a hand doesn't mean you played it well. Losing a hand doesn't mean you made a mistake.</p>

<p>Poker is a game of decisions, not outcomes. Two players can make the exact same pre-flop call — one gets lucky and wins, one gets unlucky and loses. The decision was identical. The result was random.</p>

<p><strong>Expected Value (EV)</strong> cuts through the noise. A play is +EV if, over thousands of repetitions, it makes money — regardless of what happened this time. A play is -EV if it costs money in the long run, even if you won the pot tonight.</p>

<p>Most players improve slowly because they judge their decisions by results. AI Hand Review gives you the data to judge your decisions by the only thing that actually matters: were they +EV?</p>

<h2>How to Use It After a Session</h2>

<p>The feedback is only useful if you act on it. Here's a simple post-session routine:</p>

<p><strong>1. Open Game Record after your session</strong>Your last 20 hands are logged automatically. No manual input required.</p>

<p><strong>2. Filter for your biggest mistakes</strong>Start with hands where you felt uncertain in the moment — a fold that didn't feel right, a river call you second-guessed, a spot where you weren't sure of your sizing. These are the hands most likely to have EV leaks.</p>

<p><strong>3. Run AI Hand Review on 3–5 hands</strong>Don't try to review everything. Pick a small number and go deep. Read the feedback, understand the reasoning, and note the pattern — are your mistakes concentrated pre-flop? On the river? In certain positions?</p>

<p><strong>4. Identify one leak to fix</strong>One concrete adjustment per session compounds quickly. If the AI consistently flags your river bluff frequency as too high, that's your focus for next session — not your pre-flop ranges, not your bet sizing, just that one leak.</p>

<p><strong>5. Return and test</strong>Next session, play with that adjustment in mind. Pull up the same type of hands afterward and see if the AI feedback has shifted.</p>
