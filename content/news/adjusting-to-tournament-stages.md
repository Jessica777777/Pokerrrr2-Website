<p>Just like how there’s a difference between optimal strategy for tournaments vs cash games, did you also know that you should play slightly differently depending on what part of the tournament you’re in? During a tournament, each player starts with the same amount of chips, and we keep raising the blinds and playing until only one player wins.</p>

<p>We can break these down into a few key stages, based on how much of the field is left, the payout implications for placing in different positions, etc. While you always want to play a solid strategy that’s based on math and poker logic, making a few adjustments based on tournament stages will help you to win more chips from your tournaments.</p>

<p><strong>Early Stages</strong></p>

<p>During the first part of the tournament, peoples’ stacks are relatively even, and you still have a long way to go. That’s why we want to <a href="news/protecting-your-tournament-stack.html">protect our tournament life</a> by not going all-in too much, especially when we only have thin value.</p>

<p>Of course, just by playing good poker, you’ll find yourselves going all-in sometimes—and inevitably busting sometimes as well. However, we shouldn’t seek out these spots. Basically, we want to play the early stages like a cash game, only slightly tighter.</p>

<p><strong>Middle Stages</strong></p>

<p>At the middle stages, anywhere between 50% and 20% of the field remains. At this point, you want to get your chips in with a decent edge, yet understand that you’ll still lose sometimes. At this point, we want to amass as large of a stack as possible so that we have more power in the later stages and, therefore, a great chance to win the entire tournament.</p>

<p><strong>On the Bubble</strong>

The ‘bubble’ occurs right before payouts. For instance, if the tournament pays out to the top 10%, then we hit the bubble when we’re around 15%. If you have a big stack on the bubble, apply lots of small <a href="news/poker-fundamentals-aggression.html">aggressions</a> because you want to get shorter stacks to fold. They’re more likely to give it up because they are so close to cashing and they don’t want to lose that.</p>

<p>If you’re playing a <a href="news/short-stack-strategy.html">short stack</a>, however, it’s fine to play tighter so that you can win some chips. However, whatever you do, don’t just blind out—even if you’ll get a min cash. If you just fold forever and lose all your chips to the blinds, there’s no way you’ll ever win the whole shebang.</p>

<p><strong>Late Stages</strong></p>

<p>Once we’ve crossed the bubble, the shallower stacks no longer want to play as tight. They’ve won their buy-ins back, so now it’s time to get aggressive and take a shot at winning the tournament. Don’t be afraid to shoot for the moon.</p>

<p>Apply pressure to stacks that are smaller than yours, and, most crucially, play a good, fundamentally sound strategy. At this point, it’s all about that combination and luck of</p>

<p>skill that will help you to cross the finish line.</p>
