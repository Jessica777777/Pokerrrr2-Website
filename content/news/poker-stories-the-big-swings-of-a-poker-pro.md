<p>What does it feel like to slowly grind up a<a href="news/building-up-a-bankroll.html"> </a><a href="news/building-up-a-bankroll.html">poker bankroll</a> and then, after just a few months in Vegas, be on your very last buy-in? These are the kinds of real <strong>poker stories</strong> that capture the emotional rollercoaster of professional poker life.</p>

<p><a href="https://pokerdb.thehendonmob.com/player.php?n=77323" target="_blank" rel="noopener">John O'Shea</a> is your typical poker pro. In a<a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqCDC4Pwjx0" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> </a><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqCDC4Pwjx0" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Real Stories Documentary</a>, we see the Irishman begin his poker journey by winning 150 Euros as a drunk college kid without even knowing the rules. “If I learn the rules of this game, I’ll make a fortune,” he concludes.</p>

<p><em>Photo: </em><a href="https://uk.pokernews.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><em>pokernews</em></a></p>

<p>He grinds it up online, sometimes waiting for hours on weekend nights for action-seeking fish to log in so that he can win easy money. When he finally makes day 2 of an Irish Open, it’s a big deal for him.</p>

<p>Full of confidence, he decides to head to Las Vegas for the<a href="news/how-to-prepare-for-the-world-series-of-poker.html"> </a><a href="news/how-to-prepare-for-the-world-series-of-poker.html">World Series of Poker</a>, the biggest event in the poker world. At dinner with his family the night before his departure, his mom tearfully tells him that she would much rather see him “in a proper job… even at McDonalds, anywhere.”</p>

<p>He kisses her goodbye and hops on the plane.</p>

<p><strong>The World Series</strong></p>

<p>Just a few months later, O’Shea lost almost $400,000. With his final $10,000, he bought into the main event; this is his last chance to recoup some of his losses. He’s “done everything right,” he says, even focusing on his fitness with a personal trainer, but he’s still gone home with no money.</p>

<p>“I’ve been playing for six years now and haven’t gone broke,” O’Shea says as he heads to play the main event. “So, fingers crossed.”</p>

<p>Day 1 goes smoothly, as he gets it all in with<a href="news/looking-at-pocket-aces-avoid-these-3-common-mistakes.html"> </a><a href="news/looking-at-pocket-aces-avoid-these-3-common-mistakes.html">pocket aces</a> against queens and “sends the man back to Nebraska.” Staying positive, at the start of day 2, he makes a big bet with AJ and gets called. By the river, he’s holding a flush against a<a href="news/strategies-for-sets-1.html"> </a><a href="news/strategies-for-sets-1.html">set</a>, and he wins a big pot. He’s in a good spot, so it’s time to make a run on day 3.</p>

<p>The action continues with him going all-in on a call with QQ<a href="news/pre-flop-fundamentals.html"> </a><a href="news/pre-flop-fundamentals.html">pre-flop</a> only to see that his opponent is holding aces. The flop was all low cards, the turn was a 5—and then the river: a queen. “Send him back to Italy with that one,” smirks O’Shea as he finishes day 3 with over 500k in chips.</p>

<p>That’s when his luck starts to change. Again holding queens, he calls a <a href="news/playing-against-maniacs.html">loose-aggressive</a> Frenchman and, despite having 92% <a href="news/poker-fundamentals-equity.html">equity</a>, loses on the river to a flush. Trying to claw it back with his<a href="news/how-to-play-short-stack-poker.html"> </a><a href="news/how-to-play-short-stack-poker.html">short stack</a>, he shoves all-in with A9 and gets called by AK. With only 5% equity and his tournament life on the line, the flop brings 8Q2, the turn is a jack, and a king comes on the river. Just like that, his tournament is over.</p>

<p>He may have lost, but he still cashed for $52k, giving him a profit of $42k. At least he isn’t broke. “I started with nothing and built it up,” he sighs. “The important thing is what you do when the curve is going down.”</p>

<p>At the end of the day, we see him back on his computer, playing online poker, slowly<a href="news/poker-stories-the-grind-sharpens-the-axe.html"> </a><a href="news/poker-stories-the-grind-sharpens-the-axe.html">grinding</a> his bankroll back up.</p>
