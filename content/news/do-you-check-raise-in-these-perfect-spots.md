<p>Check-raising has become a mainstay of <a href="news/want-to-win-huge-pots-increase-your-aggression.html">aggressive poker strategy</a>. Feigning weakness with a check and then responding to a bet with a raise puts the bettor under massive pressure. It can be a great way to get value from a good hand or force a better hand to fold with a bluff.</p>

<p>But it wasn’t always such a popular technique. In fact, in the early days of poker, check-raising was considered disrespectful. People thought it was so rude that some casinos, saloons, and gambling halls<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Check-raise" target="_blank" rel="noopener"> </a><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Check-raise" target="_blank" rel="noopener">even went so far as to ban the play altogether</a>.</p>

<p>These days it’s a common deceptive play, and knowing how to use it properly can give you a massive edge.</p>

<p>These are three spots that are perfect for check-raising.</p>

<p><strong>1) When someone always continuation bets</strong></p>

<p>There are some players who think they always have to bet the flop if they raised <a href="news/pre-flop-fundamentals.html">pre-flop</a>. These <a href="news/c-bet-strategy-frequency-and-sizing.html">continuation bettors</a> usually use a small bet sizing, and their predictability makes them easy to counter.</p>

<p>On boards where their range connects well, such as high cards when they’re in early position, use a large check-raise to put them in a tough spot with all of their non-premium hands. They won’t be able to call unless they have a hand, and then you’ll know to get out of the way if your cards can’t match up.</p>

<p>On the other hand, there are boards where their range connects poorly. In this situation, it’s better to use a small check-raise because they’ll still have a hard time <a href="news/defending-vs-bets.html">defending</a> against even a small raise.</p>

<p>When you win these small pots over and over again, you’ll win tons of chips.</p>

<p><strong>2) Attack weak bet sizes</strong></p>

<p>Many weak players use a <a href="news/figuring-out-pre-flop-bet-sizing.html">bet size</a> that broadcasts their hand strength. For instance, they may always bet small when they have a marginal hand or always bet large when they have a monster.</p>

<p>Punish these sizing tells by utilizing check-raises.</p>

<p>You may be holding air, but if you can tell that someone is holding a hand that isn’t great because they bet small, you can force them to fold with a check raise.</p>

<p>For instance, many small stakes players will make a small bet on the river with thin value. Punish them by going over the top with a huge check-raise, and you’ll find yourself scooping up more pots.</p>

<p><strong>3) Check-raise on boards that are great for you</strong></p>

<p>When people put money in the pot on boards where you can have monster hands and they can’t, punish them with aggression.</p>

<p>One way to do this is by focusing on off-suit combos. If you can have off-suit nutted hands in your range and your opponent can’t, then your aggression becomes that much more believable.</p>

<p>Forcing those folds is all about making a credible threat.</p>

<p>Remember to size your aggression in the same manner that you would with value hands so that the story makes sense.</p>

<p>Even if your hand is mediocre at best, knowing that a board is great for your range lets you amp up the aggression and deploy more check-raises.</p>
