<h2>What Is a Sit &amp; Go?</h2>

<p>A Sit &amp; Go is a single-table poker tournament (typically 3 to 9 players) that begins as soon as every seat is filled. Everyone starts with the same stack, blinds increase on a timer, and the last player standing takes the biggest share of the prize pool.</p>

<p><strong>SNG vs. Multi-Table Tournaments (MTT):</strong></p>

<tr><th><br></th><th><strong>Sit &amp; Go</strong></th><th><strong>MTT</strong></th></tr>

<p>Think of it this way: MTTs are marathons. <strong>Sit &amp; Go is a sprint with a poker brain.</strong></p>

<h2>Sit &amp; Go Strategy: The 3 Phases</h2>

<p>Every SNG plays out in three distinct phases. Knowing when to shift gears is what separates breakeven players from profitable ones.</p>

<h3>Phase 1: Early Game (Full Table, Low Blinds)</h3>

<p><strong>Play tight.</strong> The blinds are small relative to stacks, so there's no reason to gamble. Let the loose players knock each other out.</p>

<ul>
<li>Stick to <a href="news/best-starting-hands-in-poker-guide.html">premium hands</a>: high pocket pairs, AK, AQ</li>
<li>Avoid marginal spots — you don't need to build a big stack yet</li>
<li>Observe your opponents. Who's aggressive? Who folds too much? This information pays off later.
<br></li>
</ul>

<h3>Phase 2: Middle Game (Half the Table Left, Rising Blinds)</h3>

<p><strong>Start applying pressure.</strong> Blinds are eating into shorter stacks, and players tighten up to survive into the money. This is where you steal.</p>

<ul>
<li>Open wider from <a href="news/what-s-your-favorite-position-why-position-matters-in-nlhe.html">late position</a></li>
<li>Attack players who are clearly waiting for the money</li>
<li>Re-steal against loose openers if your stack allows it
<br></li>
</ul>

<h3>Phase 3: Bubble &amp; Heads-Up (Final 2–3 Players, High Blinds)</h3>

<p><strong>This is where SNGs are won or lost.</strong> The bubble (one spot before the money) creates massive pressure.</p>

<ul>
<li>Short stack? Know your <a href="news/how-to-play-short-stack-poker.html">push/fold ranges</a>. At 10 big blinds or less, it's shove-or-fold — no limping, no small raises.</li>
<li>Big stack? Pressure the medium stacks. They can't afford to call without a premium hand.</li>
<li>Heads-up? Aggression wins. Raise nearly every button, and don't let your opponent see free flops.
<br></li>
</ul>

<h2>Common SNG Mistakes</h2>

<ol>
<li><strong>Playing too many hands early.</strong> There's no prize for having the chip lead at level 2. Survival matters more than accumulation in Phase 1.</li>
<li><strong>Not adjusting to stack sizes.</strong> A 15 big blind stack plays completely differently from a 50 big blind stack. If you're short, stop seeing flops — start shoving.</li>
<li><strong>Overvaluing ICM late.</strong> Yes, pay jumps matter. But <a href="news/poker-tournament-strategy-for-every-stage.html">playing too passively on the bubble</a> lets aggressive players steamroll you. Pick your spots to fight back.</li>
<li><strong>Ignoring position.</strong> In a short-handed SNG, position is everything. The button is the most profitable seat at the table — use it.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions About Sit &amp; Go Poker</h2>

<p>Q1: How long does a Sit &amp; Go tournament last?</p>

<p>A: A 6-player Sit &amp; Go typically lasts 30-60 minutes, while a</p>

<p>9-player SNG runs 60-90 minutes depending on blind structure.</p>

<p>Turbo formats with faster blind levels can finish in 20-30 minutes.</p>

<p>Once registration fills and the tournament starts, blinds increase</p>

<p>every 5-10 minutes until one player has all the chips.</p>

<p>Q2: How many players do you need for a Sit &amp; Go?</p>

<p>A: Standard Sit &amp; Go formats require 6 or 9 players to start.</p>

<p>Heads-up SNGs require just 2 players, while smaller "Spin &amp; Go"</p>

<p>variants typically use 3 players. The tournament begins automatically</p>

<p>once the required number of seats fill, with no scheduled start time.</p>

<p>Q3: What's the difference between a Sit &amp; Go and a regular tournament?</p>

<p>A: A Sit &amp; Go is a single-table tournament that starts when seats</p>

<p>fill, while regular tournaments (MTTs) have scheduled start times</p>

<p>and span multiple tables. SNGs are faster (30-90 minutes vs 3+ hours)</p>

<p>and more predictable in length. SNG strategy focuses on push/fold</p>

<p>ranges and ICM, while MTT strategy involves multi-stage adjustments.</p>

<p>Q4: What's the best Sit &amp; Go strategy for beginners?</p>

<p>A: Beginners should play tight in the early stages (full table,</p>

<p>low blinds), open up gradually as players bust, and master push/fold</p>

<p>strategy for the bubble and heads-up phases. Focus on premium hands</p>

<p>in early position, steal blinds aggressively when stacks shorten,</p>

<p>and learn ICM basics — the late-game payout structure rewards</p>

<p>survival over chip accumulation.</p>
