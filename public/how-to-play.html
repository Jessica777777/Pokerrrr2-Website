No Limit Hold'em Rules

Texas Hold’em is played with two private cards and five shared community cards. Your goal is to win the pot by making the best five-card hand at showdown or getting every other player to fold.

The Basics

Each hand starts with the dealer button, which moves one seat clockwise after every hand. The two players to the left of the button post forced bets called blinds to get the action started:

Small blind

The player directly to the left of the button, who posts the smaller forced bet.

Big blind

The next player to the left, who posts the larger forced bet, typically twice the small blind.

Every player is then dealt two cards face down. These are your hole cards.

Your Options

On your turn, you can:

Fold

Give up your hand and take no further part in the current hand.

Check

Pass the action without betting, if no bet needs to be matched.

Bet

Put chips into the pot when no one has bet in the current betting round.

Call

Match the current bet.

Raise

Increase the current bet, requiring other players who wish to continue to match the raise.

All-in

Bet all your remaining chips.

The actions available depend on the betting situation. For example, you cannot check if there is a bet to call.

The Betting Rounds

Pre-flop

After everyone receives their hole cards, betting begins with the player to the left of the big blind. Players can fold, call, or raise.

Flop

Three community cards are dealt face up, followed by a round of betting.

Turn

A fourth community card is dealt face up, followed by another round of betting.

River

The fifth and final community card is dealt face up, followed by the last round of betting.

Making Your Hand

Your final hand is the best five-card combination you can make from your two hole cards and the five community cards. You can use both, one, or none of your hole cards.

Example: You hold 7♠ 7♥, and the board is 7♦ 9♣ A♠ A♦ 2♥. Your best hand is a full house, sevens full of aces (7-7-7-A-A). If the board were J-7-5-6-2 instead, your best hand would be three of a kind, sevens.

Playing the board: Sometimes, the best five-card hand is made entirely from the community cards. For example, if the board is 3-4-5-6-7 and you hold 9-9, the straight on the board beats your pair of nines. Your best hand is the five-card straight.

If no player can make a better five-card hand using their hole cards, everyone plays the board and the pot is split equally among the remaining players.

How a Hand Ends

Showdown

If two or more players remain after the final betting round, they reveal their cards. The player with the best five-card hand wins the pot. If multiple players have equally ranked hands, the pot is split among them.

Everyone folds

If you bet or raise and all other players fold, you win the pot without showing your cards.

You don't always need the best hand to win. Sometimes, the right bet is enough to take the pot. That's part of what makes No-Limit Texas Hold’em so exciting: strategy, timing, and reading your opponents can matter just as much as the cards you're dealt.
